# English humanizing decisions

Experimental editorial guidance; no native-editor calibration. Use the requested register and locale. These examples are invented teaching material.

## Start with the useful content

Edit an opener or closer when it only announces what the next sentence already says.

Before: “Let us take a moment to explore the schedule. Registration closes on Thursday.”
After: “Registration closes on Thursday.”
Keep: “Thanks for waiting. Registration closes on Thursday.” Gratitude may belong in a reply to someone who waited.

## Replace padding with the existing claim

A decorative claim of transformation adds little to a concrete feature. Preserve an actual opinion or comparative result.

Before: “The invoice archive heralds an exciting new chapter in document discovery. It includes full-text search.”
After: “The invoice archive includes full-text search.”
Keep: “I like the search box, although it misses handwritten invoices.” The reaction and limitation both matter.

Do not replace jargon with unsupported specifics. In engineering, “robust estimation,” “gating signal” and “critical section” can be precise terms.

## Prefer a direct verb when it keeps the meaning

Before: “We are in the process of reviewing the access rules.”
After: “We are reviewing the access rules.”
Keep: “The access rules are reviewed by an external auditor.” The passive can focus attention on the rules; do not invent an actor when none is known.

Keep modality and timing. “May fail” is different from “fails”; “designed to reduce errors” is different from “reduces errors.”

## Make contrast and rhythm earn their space

Before: “This is not merely a list, but a revolution in planning. The list groups repairs by building.”
After: “The list groups repairs by building.”
Keep: “The fee covers storage, not retrieval.” Both halves define the offer.

Merge repeated wording only when every distinct item survives. Dashes, fragments, three-item lists and repeated openings can be intentional. A technical checklist needs parallel structure; do not make it less scannable to vary rhythm.

## Leave evidence boundaries visible

Before: “A few reviewers expect demand to rise, a truly remarkable testament to the product's potential.”
After: “A few reviewers expect demand to rise.”
Keep the expectation as their view. If the review needs a source, request it outside the rewritten passage. Do not upgrade it to “Demand will rise.”

A date, research limitation or apology can serve the reader. Remove only redundant framing; keep the reason an assertion is uncertain.
