# finance — Financial and investor communication

Scope: descriptions of financial results, operating metrics, investor updates and
existing product-risk explanations. This is editorial support, not investment advice,
accounting audit or jurisdiction-specific regulatory certification.

## Editing decisions

- Keep each amount attached to its entity, reporting period, currency and unit.
  Preserve consolidated/standalone, gross/net, adjusted/unadjusted and actual/forecast
  distinctions when supplied. Do not invent missing accounting bases.
- Preserve year-on-year versus sequential comparisons and percentages versus
  percentage points or basis points. Do not silently recalculate or convert units.
- A historical return is not a promised return. Retain loss exposure, assumptions,
  fees, liquidity conditions and unaudited/provisional qualifications where present.
- Explain an unfamiliar metric using the supplied definition. Do not replace a
  precise measure with “业绩亮眼”, or add impressive financial terms without purpose.
- Lead with the supported result and its basis when useful. Keep forecasts and
  scenario assumptions adjacent; “预计”“可能”“未审计” carry meaning.
- If two supplied figures conflict, report that conflict. Polishing cannot choose
  which number is true. Verification needs the identified source table or record.

Original example (edit): “在复杂多变的市场环境下，公司取得了令人瞩目的进展，
2026 年上半年营业收入为人民币 800 万元，同比增长 6%，数据未经审计。” →
“2026 年上半年，公司营业收入为人民币 800 万元，同比增长 6%；数据未经审计。”

Preserve: “毛利率从 20% 升至 23%，提高 3 个百分点；该结果不代表未来收益。”
Do not report a 3% relative increase or delete the future-return qualification.

Rationale: the [SEC's Plain Writing Initiative](https://www.sec.gov/about/plain-writing-initiative)
supports understandable investor communication. The profile uses that writing
principle, not US rules as worldwide compliance requirements. Its financial
preservation rules must still be checked against the user's actual source.
