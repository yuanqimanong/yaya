# academic — Research writing

Scope: abstracts, supplied methods/results, discussion, limitations and literature
review prose. General research-reporting support is not a complete discipline or
journal style guide. Use any supplied journal requirements within the requested task.

## Editing decisions

- Keep the research question, design, sample, setting and outcome scope. Separate
  observations from interpretation and proposed explanations.
- Calibrate verbs to the supplied evidence. An association does not “prove” a cause;
  absence of evidence is not proof of no effect. Preserve relevant qualifications.
- Keep quotations, citation identifiers and attribution attached to the supported
  claim. Never invent a reference, DOI, sample, method, result or author credential.
- Improve the relation between purpose, method, result and conclusion where needed.
  Do not impose IMRAD headings on every paragraph, review or essay.
- Retain limitations and contrary findings. Formal prose may be direct and readable;
  nominalizations, passive voice and jargon are choices, not proof of scholarship.
- Missing evidence or methodological detail stays missing and, where consequential,
  is identified separately. Do not polish away uncertainty to make a result stronger.

Original example (edit): “It is important to note that our interview study offers
preliminary indications of this pattern among 18 participants at one site.” →
“Our interview study offers preliminary indications of this pattern among 18
participants at one site.”

Preserve: “该观察性研究发现相关性，但不能据此推断因果关系 [12]。” Do not detach
the citation, remove the design limit or turn the finding into universal causation.

Rationale: [ICMJE's manuscript guidance](https://icmje.org/recommendations/browse/manuscript-preparation/preparing-for-submission.html)
connects conclusions to evidence and limitations. It concerns medical journals;
Yaya borrows those general reporting principles without claiming medical expertise
or that its format applies to every discipline.
